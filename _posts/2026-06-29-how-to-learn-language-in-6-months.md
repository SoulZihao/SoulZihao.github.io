Learning how to learn—I call it **meta-learning**.

Chinese students work incredibly hard, but high-impact achievements are rare. One of the main reasons, IMO, is that **they don’t know what they don’t know**. They know *that* they should work hard, but they don’t know *how* they should work hard.

These meta-level methodologies are what we refer to as mental principles, or **“心法”** in Chinese (which I think is a much more appropriate term).

> 当你掌握了“心法”，你的效率会呈线性提升（$n$ times）；而当你掌握了“心法的心法”，你的效率将会发生指数级跃升（$n^2$ times）。

So, learning how to learn is definitely more important than learning itself.

## 🧠 Core Principles

*   **Focus on language content that is relevant to you.**
    > *Quote: "A person who is drowning cannot learn to swim."*
    > 
    > 💡 **My Reflection:** I used to think this meant adjusting the difficulty level. But historically and psychologically, it means when you are overwhelmed by anxiety or flooded with incomprehensible noise, your brain enters survival mode and completely shuts down its acquisition mechanism.
*   **Use your new language as a tool to communicate from Day 1.**
    > 💡 **My Reflection:** This means using the language as a tool in your relevant field. For me, that field is **Computer Science**. Reading docs and writing code in English from day one genuinely works.
*   **Unconscious acquisition through message comprehension.**
    > Comprehensible input is vastly superior to formal, brute-force grammar study. The only thing your brain should focus on is *understanding the meaning*.
*   **Language is physical training.**
    > It's not just mental storage; it's muscle memory. You must practice moving your facial muscles and speaking.
*   **The emotional state makes a massive difference.**
    > Stay happy and relaxed during the process. Don't beat yourself up if you don't understand 100% of it. Tolerance of ambiguity is a superpower.

---

## 🛠️ Actionable Tactics

1.  **Brain Soaking:** Listen a lot. Immerse yourself in the sound patterns.
2.  **Meaning First:** Focus on getting the meaning first, utilizing non-verbal cues and context.
3.  **Start Mixing:** Don't wait for perfection. Start mixing words to create primitive sentences.
4.  **Focus on the Core:** Master the core vocabulary. 3,000 words give you 98% coverage of daily communication.
5.  **Find a Language Parent:** Someone who will patiently listen to you and try to understand your broken language. (that’s difficult for me😭)
6.  **Copy the Face:** Imitate how native speakers move their facial muscles. Crucial for speaking, though I haven't paid enough attention to it yet.
7.  **Direct Connection:** Connect words directly to mental images, completely skipping the internal translation step.
